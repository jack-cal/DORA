SUMUWW · current height-9000 protocol

Current implementation: PC 0.57.40 / Android 0.6.89 · 2026-10-24. Recovery was introduced in PC 0.57.34 / Android 0.6.84.

History, money and compatibility

The genesis and all pre-9000 consensus rules remain unchanged. Valid history up to 8999 is accepted even when an old peer advertises an invalid continuation. Each body is validated and committed separately; rejecting a later body does not roll back the accepted prefix. Existing unspent coins remain subject to their original owner signatures and maturity. A peer's software age alone is not evidence that a transaction or block is invalid.

The first 150000 heights have a subsidy of 50 SMW; at 150000 it becomes 25 SMW and halves every 210000 heights, using integer atoms (100000000 atoms = 1 SMW). Nominal issuance is 17999999.97690000 SMW; excluding the unspendable genesis reward it is 17999949.97690000 SMW. Coinbase maturity remains 100 blocks.

Difficulty and mandatory block data

Below 8064 the original 2016-block adjustment applies. From 8064 through 8999, DGW v3 recalculates every block with the preceding 24 blocks on its own branch. At 9000, 9010, 9020… it recalculates using the same 24-block algorithm; between these heights the parent nBits is retained. The target interval is 600 seconds. Nodes reject unexpected nBits even if the supplied hash meets an easier target.

Every block at height 9000 or above must contain exactly one zero-value coinbase output with script 6a25 || 534d574c01 || producer_ed25519_public_key_32_bytes. The native header remains 80 bytes and uses SHA-256d PoW. Old miners which omit this output are rejected at 9000, not at a random later height. Miners from an intermediate revision can still produce blocks which satisfy all current rules; there is no remotely trustworthy executable-version blacklist. A previous probability policy's attachment is rejected without invalidating an otherwise valid native block. A wrongly recalculated difficulty is rejected independently.

Asynchronous connection evidence

PoW completion, native block acceptance and propagation do not require witness or randomness replies. A source producer schedules a job for its block A and a specific direct child B. The job keeps the exact A/B target across retries. A verified threshold beacon selects a connection event when its 256-bit output is less than floor(2^256/5). This is a 20% event probability, not a five-block schedule or a guarantee that 20% of blocks obtain completed evidence.

For a new job, the producer probes its entire discovered identity inventory through authenticated nonce echoes, including relayed participants. At least three identities must respond before the job proceeds to the verified beacon and its 20% event test. The producer then samples three responding identities without replacement, using rejection sampling. Silent contacts remain in the discovery inventory for later attempts. Responses are local availability observations, not consensus membership or a guaranteed future signature. There are no registration fees, membership admissions, exits, one-person checks, per-IP lottery quotas or fixed candidate-count/prefix/hop limits. Discovery continues through relays. Unknown/offline nodes cannot be guaranteed to appear; candidate omission by a malicious producer and a global uniform census are not proven. One operator can run multiple identities. This design does not provide Sybil resistance based on unique human identity.

Each of the three witnesses signs the canonical network/parent/height/slot/ A/B/team/beacon message with Ed25519. Each 64-byte signature is split into two 32-byte halves. The three front halves form 96 bytes; the three back halves form another 96 bytes. Each bundle uses zlib only if shorter, otherwise raw encoding. Signature bytes are generally incompressible; compression is not guaranteed. Both halves reconstruct all three signatures during verification. They are signature evidence, not a newly generated private key.

The source producer signs the paired attachment and B's producer signs a receipt binding that same attachment. Only a complete paired proof earns a score. Proofs are persisted in link_proofs and relayed separately from immutable PoW bytes. They refer to exact A/B hashes; completing them never changes either block hash. Missing halves, forged signatures, altered target hashes and wrong ancestry fail verification. Late nodes fetch the blocks, anchored randomness transcript and complete proofs, including conflict evidence on side branches, and independently reconstruct and verify signatures. Some jobs can remain incomplete indefinitely.

Job progress and counters

A beacon setup record prepares a randomness generation. One setup can serve many A/B jobs; it is not one attempted proof. Local job counters separate:

Counter Meaning
Awaiting lottery Participant or beacon preparation has not produced a final event result
Not selected The verified 20% event test did not select this job; no completed proof is expected
Selected pending The job was selected but still lacks required signatures, a receipt or verification
Selected complete This node's selected job completed and was verified and stored
Stored complete proofs All complete proofs stored locally, including proofs received from peers

The four local job outcomes sum to the local job total. Setup records are a separate population. For example, 11 setup records and 9 selected jobs do not imply 2 completed proofs. Stored proofs and local completed jobs can also differ. An awaiting-lottery count does not mean that the 20% selection has failed.

Each valid witness reply is persisted immediately, reverified and reused after a retry or restart. Corrupt saved replies are discarded and requested again. The job records its phase, missing witnesses and errors. Existing selected jobs keep the exact message and team, including signature locks; the repair cannot replace missing original witnesses or make old incomplete proofs complete. Completion also requires the source attachment and the child producer's receipt.

Randomness and availability limits

The experimental randomness protocol uses Feldman DKG with encrypted shares and threshold BLS output. Its public setup is committed in an ordinary PoW coinbase after activation. A connection uses a setup anchored before its source block. Any threshold of valid responses reconstructs the same result. Missing responses below that threshold leave evidence pending; PoW continues. Initial setup still requires every listed dealer's contribution and a readiness quorum. Background recovery probes discovered contacts, retries failed DKG attempts and prepares a fresh key with responsive participants. A replacement is bound to the previous setup block and an ancestor round at least 12 blocks later, then committed in a subsequent ordinary PoW block. Old proofs keep their original setup; the new key applies only to later source blocks. Lost old secrets are not reconstructed. Recovery needs enough responsive participants, eventual delivery and a mined commitment. Mining never waits for the recovery process. See BEACON_RECOVERY.md.

Chain selection and conflicts

Valid cumulative work is always primary. At equal work after activation, count nonconflicting proven A/B edges contained in each branch; the greater count wins, then the smaller internal tip-hash integer wins. Duplicate proofs for one edge do not multiply its score. If distinct edges occupy one parent/height slot, all edges in that slot score zero, including when all three witnesses double-sign. Honest local keys also refuse a second different message for one slot.

There is no arrival-order finality lock. Late evidence or newly discovered conflicts can change an equal-work choice; a valid greater-work branch wins. Hidden evidence is not proven absent. These rules do not guarantee irreversible finality, majority-hashrate immunity or prevention of all forks. Broadcast and verification consume CPU, disk and network time; asynchronous scheduling does not guarantee zero resource contention or unlimited hostile-load resilience.

Other retained features and migration

Exact-template P2WSH SHA256/preimage/CLTV spends activate at 9100. Ordinary P2WPKH transfers, existing wallet encryption, 34 UI languages, icons and layout remain. The HTLC extension is not a general script VM or an automatic swap service.

The policy identifier is sumuww-postpow-links-9000-v2; new signing transport uses protocol 5, with native-only fallback for compatible older peers. The preactivation v1 database marker may migrate without rewriting blocks or UTXOs. A database already containing incompatible post-activation history is refused for explicit review. Do not delete it or reset a wallet to bypass this check.

The shipped implementation and isolated test evidence are not an independent security audit or proof of worldwide decentralization. The original laboratory tests used isolated networks. On 24 October 2026, read-only checks observed a local active chain beyond heights 9000 and 9100. That snapshot does not establish network-wide adoption, uninterrupted operation or successful completion of repaired connection jobs on the public network.
